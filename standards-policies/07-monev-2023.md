# 7. Notes on Monev (2023)

Veselin Monev, "Data Leakage Prevention in ISO 27001: Compliance and Implementation",
InfoTech-2023 (IEEE). A short practitioner paper on the DLP control introduced in
ISO/IEC 27001:2022, from two angles: demonstrating compliance, and implementing it.

## What the paper contributes

### 1. A working definition of DLP

The author notes there is no consensus definition and that "data leakage prevention" and
"data loss prevention" are used interchangeably. Drawing on ISO/IEC 27002:2022, he defines
DLP as organizational, technical, people and physical controls that detect and prevent
unauthorized disclosure or extraction of sensitive information by individuals or systems,
then decomposes it (his Table 1):

| Dimension | His position |
|---|---|
| Goals | Mainly confidentiality and privacy |
| Functions | Mainly detect and prevent |
| Scope | Mainly digital data on networks, devices, systems; paper and verbal secondary |
| Threats | Internal and external, human and system: insider misconduct, social engineering, hackers, malware |
| Supporting documents | Existing policies — classification, acceptable use, access control, cryptography, incident management |

He departs deliberately from NIST SP 800-137's leakage/theft split, which he considers too
narrow and inconsistent with ISO 27002. A secondary point: many organizations already
operate DLP-type controls under other names (encryption, access management, classification).

### 2. Four compliance approaches

Three steps are treated as mandatory regardless: add data leakage to the risk catalogue,
assess the risk, update the Statement of Applicability. Beyond that:

- Controls already adequate:
  1. prepare arguments and evidence for the auditor, minimal effort;
  2. update documentation to raise procedural maturity;
  3. improve or expand controls using ISO 27002, the Secure Controls Framework and vendor
     guidance.
- Controls missing or inadequate:
  4. close the gaps with a planned rollout.

### 3. Three implementation approaches

1. Follow ISO/IEC 27002 directly (classification, channel monitoring, blocking actions,
   supporting policies).
2. Deploy a dedicated DLP product, used as compliance evidence; capabilities listed include
   visibility of data at rest/in motion/in use, incident context, automated response, user
   coaching, central policy management, behaviour analytics.
3. Map existing framework controls onto DLP — his Table 2 groups eight areas: weakness
   management, security in projects, monitoring and event handling (SIEM/XDR/NDR/DLP),
   third-party risk, perimeter security, control of export channels, access control with
   privileged-user monitoring, secure media disposal.

## What to attribute to whom

These are **Monev's constructs, not ISO's**:

| Construct | Status in ISO/IEC 27002:2022 |
|---|---|
| "Aspects of DLP" table | Not in the standard. ISO has five formal control **attributes** (clause 4.2); his goals/functions/scope overlap with them. His "privacy" goal is not among the ISO attributes, which list confidentiality only. |
| Four compliance approaches | Not in 27002 at all. 27001 only requires risk assessment (6.1.2), risk treatment + SoA (6.1.3), documented information (7.5). The scenarios and approaches are his practitioner advice. |
| Three implementation approaches | Not presented as such. Approach 1 is just the standard; approach 2 is half supported (ISO says DLP tools should be used and lists three functions — the richer capability list comes from vendor documentation); approach 3 is his own, the nearest ISO equivalent being Annex A of 27002, *Using attributes*, plus the cybersecurity-concept attribute aligned with ISO/IEC TS 27110. |
| Threat list (malware, social engineering, APIs, cloud platforms) | ISO says only "individuals or systems", plus adversary espionage. |
| Response actions (escalation, restoring, user coaching) | From vendor material; ISO names quarantine, blocking, alerting, and deception. |

## Accuracy note

The paper states that NIST refers to intentional data theft as "data loss". SP 800-137
does not: it uses "data loss" as the umbrella and "data theft" for the intentional case.
His underlying argument still stands in weaker form — ISO's "leakage" is broader than
NIST's — but the structure is misdescribed. Cite the NIST text directly rather than this
summary of it.

## Usefulness

Conceptual and compliance-oriented; no empirical evaluation, and the control mapping is a
proposal rather than a validated framework. Strongest uses: the structured definition, and
the leakage-vs-loss terminology argument when positioning DLP formally. For implementation
detail, go to ISO/IEC 27002 itself or NIST SP 1800-28/29.
