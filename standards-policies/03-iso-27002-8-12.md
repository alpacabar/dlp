# 3. ISO/IEC 27002:2022, control 8.12 — data leakage prevention

Paraphrased from ISO/IEC 27002:2022, 8.12 (p. 100) and ISO/IEC 27001:2022, Annex A.
For the normative wording, read the clause in a licensed copy of the standard.

## Status

New in the 2022 revision (ISO/IEC 27002 Annex B marks 8.12 as new; it has no 2013
counterpart). In ISO/IEC 27001:2022 it appears in Annex A as a one-line "shall"
statement, so it must be considered in the Statement of Applicability and either
implemented or excluded with justification.

## Attributes

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Control type | Preventive, Detective |
| Information security properties | Confidentiality |
| Cybersecurity concepts | Protect, Detect |
| Operational capabilities | Information protection |
| Security domains | Protection, Defence |

Note: confidentiality only — integrity and availability are not claimed for this control.

## Control and purpose

- **Control:** apply data leakage prevention measures to systems, networks and any other
  devices that process, store or transmit sensitive information.
- **Purpose:** detect and prevent unauthorized disclosure and extraction of information
  by individuals or systems.

The definition is **intent-neutral**: the standard does not split accidental from
deliberate disclosure. Both fall under "leakage". This matters for terminology — see
[06-iso-vs-nist.md](06-iso-vs-nist.md).

## Organizational measures

1. Identify and classify the information to be protected (examples given: personal
   information, pricing models, product designs).
2. Monitor the channels through which data leaks (email, file transfers, mobile devices,
   portable storage devices).
3. Act to prevent leakage, e.g. quarantining email that contains sensitive information.

## What DLP tools are expected to do

1. Identify and monitor sensitive information at risk of disclosure, including
   unstructured data on user systems.
2. Detect disclosure, e.g. uploads to untrusted third-party cloud services, or email.
3. Block user actions or network transmissions that would expose sensitive information,
   e.g. copying database entries into a spreadsheet.

## Further guidance

- Decide whether copy/paste and uploads to outside services, devices and media need to be
  restricted; if so, allow viewing and manipulation of remotely held data while preventing
  export beyond the organization's control.
- Where export is needed, the **data owner approves** it and users are held accountable.
- Screenshots and photographs of the screen are addressed through terms and conditions of
  use, training and auditing — not technology.
- Backups of sensitive information must be protected (encryption, access control, physical
  protection of the media).
- DLP should also consider adversary intelligence gathering (geopolitical, human,
  financial, commercial, scientific). Suggested responses include deception —
  substituting false information, reverse social engineering, honeypots.

## Other information

- DLP tools identify data, monitor its use and movement, and act (alerting users to risky
  behaviour, blocking transfers to portable storage).
- **Legal caveat (important for privacy work):** DLP inherently monitors personnel
  communications and online activity, and by extension external parties' messages. Legal
  concerns must be considered before deployment — privacy, data protection, employment,
  interception of data, telecommunications law.
- DLP is supported by topic-specific policies on access control and secure document
  management (the clause cites 5.12 and 5.15).

> Cross-reference caution: that sentence cites 5.12 and 5.15, but in the ISO/IEC 27001:2022
> Annex A list, 5.12 is *classification of information* and 5.15 is *access control*.
> Verify the numbering yourself before repeating the cross-reference in a paper.

## Why this control matters for privacy research

8.12 is a rare case of a security standard stating, in its own text, that the control
itself creates a privacy risk and is constrained by privacy and employment law. That
makes it a clean motivation for privacy-preserving monitoring: pseudonymised identifiers,
metadata rather than content, and minimisation in what the DLP system stores.
